geometry.hpp

Day 1 — vector/matrix math foundation

2D vector dot & cross product

Cross product in 2D returns a scalar: positive if the second vector is counter-clockwise of the first.
$$ \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = a_x b_x + a_y b_y \qquad \mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b} = a_x b_y - a_y b_x $$
Vec2::dot, Vec2::cross

Vector rotation

Standard 2D rotation matrix applied to a vector by angle $a$.
$$ \begin{aligned} x' &= x\cos a - y\sin a \\ y' &= x\sin a + y\cos a \end{aligned} $$
Vec2::rotated

Norm, normalization, angle

$$ \lVert \mathbf{v} \rVert = \sqrt{x^2+y^2} \qquad \hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{\lVert \mathbf{v} \rVert} \qquad \theta = \operatorname{atan2}(y, x) $$
Vec2::norm, Vec2::normalized, Vec2::angle

Point-to-segment distance

Project point $p$ onto segment $ab$, clamp the parameter $t$ to $[0,1]$, then measure distance to the clamped point.
$$ t = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{a})\cdot(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})}{\lVert \mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a} \rVert^2},\ 0,\ 1\right) \qquad d = \lVert \mathbf{p} - (\mathbf{a} + t(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})) \rVert $$
distance_to_segment

Angle wrapping

Keeps an angle in $[-\pi, \pi]$ by repeatedly adding/subtracting $2\pi$.
$$ a_{\text{wrapped}} \in [-\pi, \pi], \quad a_{\text{wrapped}} = a - 2\pi k \ \text{for integer } k $$
wrap_angle

Degrees → radians

$$ \text{rad} = \text{deg} \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} $$
deg2rad

World ↔ ego frame transforms

Ego frame: $x$ forward, $y$ left, origin at the car, heading $\psi$ (yaw).
$$ \mathbf{p}_{\text{ego}} = R(-\psi)\,(\mathbf{p}_{\text{world}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{car}}) \qquad \mathbf{p}_{\text{world}} = R(\psi)\,\mathbf{p}_{\text{ego}} + \mathbf{p}_{\text{car}} $$
to_ego_frame, to_world_frame

2×2 matrix multiplication

Used for Kalman filter state-transition / covariance propagation.
$$ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a' & b' \\ c' & d' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} aa'+bc' & ab'+bd' \\ ca'+dc' & cb'+dd' \end{pmatrix} $$
Mat2::operator*

vehicle.hpp

Day 2 — kinematic bicycle model (the plant)

Kinematic bicycle model

No slip, point mass, simplified steering. $L$ = wheelbase, $\delta$ = steering angle, $a$ = acceleration, $v$ = speed, $\psi$ = yaw.
$$ \begin{aligned} \dot{x} &= v \cos\psi \\ \dot{y} &= v \sin\psi \\ \dot{\psi} &= \frac{v}{L}\tan\delta \\ \dot{v} &= a \end{aligned} $$
Vehicle::step

Command & state clamping

$$ \begin{aligned} a &\in [-a_{\max},\ a_{\max}] \\ \delta &\in [-\delta_{\max},\ \delta_{\max}] \\ v &\in [0,\ v_{\max}] \end{aligned} $$
Vehicle::step (std::clamp calls)

controller.hpp

Day 8 — pure pursuit (lateral) + PID (longitudinal)

PID controller

Classic proportional–integral–derivative loop tracking a speed setpoint. Integral term is clamped for anti-windup.
$$ u(t) = K_p\, e(t) + K_i \int e(t)\,dt + K_d\,\frac{de(t)}{dt} $$
Discretized in code (backward-difference derivative):
$$ u_k = K_p e_k + K_i I_k + K_d\,\frac{e_k - e_{k-1}}{\Delta t}, \qquad I_k = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(I_{k-1} + e_k \Delta t,\ -I_{\lim},\ I_{\lim}\right) $$
PID::step

Pure pursuit — lookahead distance

$$ L_d = \max(L_{d,\min},\ k \cdot v) $$
PurePursuit::steer

Pure pursuit — steering angle

Inverse bicycle-model geometry: $\alpha$ = angle (in ego frame) to the lookahead target, $L$ = wheelbase.
$$ \delta = \operatorname{atan2}\!\left(2L\sin\alpha,\ L_d\right) $$
PurePursuit::steer

planner.hpp

Day 7 — lattice planner: candidate paths, cost, prediction

Lateral blend (candidate path shaping)

Smoothly blends from the current lateral offset $\text{lat}_0$ to a target offset over a fixed blend distance, so paths are drivable rather than a step change.
$$ \text{blend} = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{k \cdot ds \cdot \text{spacing}}{\text{blend}_m},\ 0,\ 1\right), \qquad \text{lat}(k) = \operatorname{lerp}(\text{lat}_0,\ \text{off},\ \text{blend}) $$
LatticePlanner::build_and_score

1. ds

ds is the distance between successive path samples.

For example:

point 0 ---- 0.5m ---- point 1 ---- 0.5m ---- point 2

Then:

ds = 0.5 m

So:

$$ k \cdot ds $$

gives approximately how far along the path we are. For example:

kdsdistance
00.5 m0 m
10.5 m0.5 m
20.5 m1.0 m
30.5 m1.5 m
40.5 m2.0 m

The important conceptual part is:

k × ds × spacing

represents progress through the blend.

2. blend_m

blend_m is the total distance over which you want the transition to happen.

Suppose:

blend_m = 10 m

You want:

current offset = 0 m
target offset  = 2 m

and you want to transition from 0 → 2 m over 10 m. Conceptually:

          10 m
<------------------------------->

offset
  2m |                        •
     |                    •
     |                •
     |            •
     |        •
  0m |•
     +-------------------------------> distance
     0                          10m

The fraction of the transition completed is:

$$ \text{blend} = \frac{\text{distance traveled}}{\text{blend distance}} $$

Put everything together

Imagine:

lat0  = 0 m
off     = 2 m
blend_m = 10 m

As the vehicle/path progresses:

Past blend_m, the clamp holds blend at 1.0, so lat(k) stays pinned at the target offset — the transition doesn't overshoot.

Path cost function

Total cost is a weighted sum of lateral-offset penalty and clearance penalty (plus a large fixed penalty for going off-track or colliding).
$$ C = w_{\text{offset}} \cdot \text{off}^2 \;+\; \sum_k P_{\text{clearance}}(k) \;+\; \begin{cases} 10^6 & \text{if off-track} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
$$ P_{\text{clearance}} = \begin{cases} 500 & \text{clearance} < 0 \\ w_{\text{clr}} \cdot (\text{safe} - \text{clr})^2 & 0 \le \text{clearance} < \text{safe} \\ 0 & \text{clearance} \ge \text{safe} \end{cases} $$
LatticePlanner::build_and_score

Target speed from clearance

$$ r = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{\text{clearance}_{\min}}{\text{clearance}_{\text{safe}}},\ 0,\ 1\right), \qquad v_{\text{target}} = \operatorname{lerp}(v_{\text{crawl}},\ v_{\text{cruise}},\ r) $$
LatticePlanner::build_and_score

Obstacle motion prediction

Two modes: straight-line extrapolation, or road-aware sliding along the centerline (keeping the same lateral offset).
$$ \mathbf{p}_{\text{future}}(t) = \begin{cases} \mathbf{p}_0 + \mathbf{v}_0\, t & \text{straight-line} \\[4pt] \text{centerline}(s_0 + \frac{v_{\parallel}\, t}{\text{spacing}}) + \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \text{lat}_0 & \text{road-aware} \end{cases} $$
LatticePlanner::build_and_score (predict lambda)

9. Full numerical example

Suppose:

current longitudinal position:
s0 = 100

lateral offset:
lat0 = 2 m

speed along road:
v∥ = 10 m/s

prediction time:
t = 2 s

The obstacle travels:

$$ 10 \times 2 = 20m $$

So:

$$ s_{\text{future}} = 100 + 20 = 120m $$

Suppose:

centerline(120) = (50, 30)

and the normal vector is:

$$ \hat{n} = (0.8, 0.6) $$

The lateral displacement is:

$$ \begin{aligned} \hat{n}\,\text{lat}_0 &= (0.8, 0.6)(2) \\ &= (1.6, 1.2) \end{aligned} $$

Therefore:

$$ p_{\text{future}} = (50, 30) + (1.6, 1.2) $$
\( p_{\text{future}} = (51.6,\ 31.2) \)

So the prediction says:

In 2 seconds, the obstacle will be 20 m farther along the road while remaining 2 m from the centerline.

tracker.hpp

Day 6 — Kalman filter (constant-velocity, per-axis) & data association

Kalman filter — predict step

State $x = [\text{position}, \text{velocity}]^T$, constant-velocity model. $F$ is the state-transition matrix, $Q$ is process noise.
$$ \mathbf{x} = F\mathbf{x}, \qquad P = FPF^T + Q, \qquad F = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \Delta t \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} $$
Kalman1D::predict

Discrete white-noise-acceleration process noise

$$ Q = q \begin{pmatrix} \Delta t^4/4 & \Delta t^3/2 \\ \Delta t^3/2 & \Delta t^2 \end{pmatrix} $$
Kalman1D::predict

Kalman filter — update step (generic)

$H$ selects which state component is measured, $R$ is measurement noise, $K$ is the Kalman gain.
$$ \begin{aligned} y &= z - H\mathbf{x} \\ S &= HPH^T + R \\ K &= PH^T S^{-1} \\ \mathbf{x} &= \mathbf{x} + Ky \\ P &= (I - KH)P \end{aligned} $$
With a 2-state filter and a scalar measurement, $S$ is a scalar and "matrix inverse" is just division — hence no linear algebra library needed.
Kalman1D::update_position ($H=[1\ 0]$), Kalman1D::update_velocity ($H=[0\ 1]$)

Position uncertainty (σ)

$$ \sigma_{\text{pos}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\operatorname{Var}(x) + \operatorname{Var}(y)\right)} $$
Track::pos_sigma

Association gate

The acceptance radius for matching a new detection to an existing track grows with the sensor's own measurement uncertainty $\sigma_{\text{det}}$.
$$ \text{gate} = \text{gate}_{m} + 2\sigma_{\text{det}} $$
Tracker::update

world.hpp

Day 4 — ground-truth simulation, course geometry

Course parameterization & interpolation

Arc-length parameter $s$ is a fractional waypoint index; linear interpolation between neighboring waypoints.
$$ \mathbf{p}(s) = \operatorname{lerp}\!\big(\mathbf{p}_{\lfloor s \rfloor},\ \mathbf{p}_{\lfloor s \rfloor + 1},\ s - \lfloor s \rfloor\big) $$
Course::point_at

Tangent, normal, heading

$$ \hat{\mathbf{t}}(s) = \widehat{\mathbf{p}(s+\tfrac12) - \mathbf{p}(s)}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{n}}(s) = R(\pi/2)\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}(s), \qquad \psi(s) = \angle \hat{\mathbf{t}}(s) $$
Course::tangent_at, normal_at, heading_at

Signed lateral offset from centerline

Positive = left of the direction of travel (via the 2D cross product).
$$ \text{lat}(\mathbf{p}) = \hat{\mathbf{t}}(i) \times (\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}_i), \qquad i = \arg\min_j \lVert \mathbf{p}_j - \mathbf{p} \rVert $$
Course::lateral_offset, Course::nearest_index

Toy adaptive cruise control (traffic)

Each actor matches the speed of whoever is directly ahead in its lane, with finite acceleration/deceleration.
$$ v_{\text{target}} = \min_{\text{leaders}} v_{\text{leader}}, \qquad v_{\text{cur}}' = \operatorname{clamp}\big(v_{\text{target}},\ v_{\text{cur}} - a_{\max}\Delta t,\ v_{\text{cur}} + a_{\max}\Delta t\big) $$
World::step

Crash detection

$$ \text{off-track: } |\text{lat}(\mathbf{p}_{\text{ego}})| + r_{\text{ego}} > w_{\text{half}} \qquad\qquad \text{collision: } \lVert \mathbf{p}_{\text{obs}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{ego}} \rVert < r_{\text{obs}} + r_{\text{ego}} $$
World::step

render.hpp

Day 9 — world-to-pixel projection & color blending

World → pixel projection

Maps a world-frame point into the terminal's half-block pixel grid ($w \times ph$), flipping $y$ since screen rows increase downward.
$$ \begin{aligned} \text{col} &= \operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{p_x - x_{\min}}{x_{\max}-x_{\min}}(w-1)\right) \\[4pt] \text{row} &= \operatorname{round}\!\left((ph-1) - \frac{p_y - y_{\min}}{y_{\max}-y_{\min}}(ph-1)\right) \end{aligned} $$
Renderer::to_pixel, Renderer::to_world (inverse)

Linear color blend

$$ c = c_1 + (c_2 - c_1)\,t, \qquad t \in [0,1] $$
mix(RGB, RGB, double)
